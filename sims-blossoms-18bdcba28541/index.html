02 Fix today

Breaks a promise at the till

Google is promising your customers free shipping that your checkout does not honour

What your site saysAED 199
What Google saysAED 149

The description Google reads off your homepage ends with "Free shipping above AED 149." Every place a shopper can see, the banner, the collection pages, your FAQ, your shipping policy, says 199. The 149 exists in one place only, and it is the one place you cannot see without viewing the source.

So I tested it the way a customer would. Your Ellie Zircon earrings are AED 149, reduced from 165. I put them in the basket and went through to the checkout:

Your checkout, 11 September 2026 Subtotal AED 149  ·  Shipment Flat rate: AED 15  ·  Total AED 164

Somebody found you in a search that said free shipping over 149, picked the item that costs exactly 149, and got charged fifteen dirhams at the last screen. That is the moment carts get abandoned, and it is worse than an ordinary shipping charge because you told them otherwise before they started. I stopped at the totals. No order was placed.

It is one line of text in your site settings. Either change the description to 199 or drop your threshold to 149 and mean it.

03 Costing you sales

Hidden until too late

Tabby only appears after they have decided to buy

Your homepage advertises Buy Now Pay Later via Tabby. Your checkout offers it properly, alongside cards and Pay in Person. Your product pages never mention it once. I checked the Ellie earrings page and the words Tabby, pay later and instalments do not appear anywhere on it.

Instalments do their work at the moment somebody is looking at a 400 dirham necklace and hesitating, not three screens later when they have already committed. Every jewellery store in Dubai that sells on Tabby puts "or 4 payments of AED 100" under the price. Yours is the only one keeping it a secret until checkout.

Too much to fill in

Your checkout asks for the address twice

  • Fields visible on a phone27
  • Fields they must fill before paying14
  • Of those, a second full address block6

Billing name, billing address, billing city, billing phone, then shipping name, shipping address, shipping city, shipping phone. The same person, typing the same thing twice, on a phone, for a pair of earrings. Almost every order you take goes to the address it is billed to, so the second block should be hidden behind a "deliver somewhere else" tick.

Questions with no answer

Your product pages answer none of the questions a jewellery buyer has

On the Ellie earrings page there is no size or measurement guide, no customer reviews, and nothing about the metal beyond the collection name. For a ring or a choker, size is the question that decides whether somebody buys or closes the tab, because they cannot try it on and they do not want the hassle of sending it back.

You have ratings in your site's data already, so the reviews exist somewhere. They are not on the page where they would do the work.

04 Worth tidying

Slow on a phone

206 images load on your homepage and 166 of them load whether anyone scrolls or not

  • Images on the homepage206
  • Waiting until they are needed40
  • Sent at more than three times the size shown110
  • Largest single file797 KB

4.1 MB on a phone, nearly 3 MB of it pictures. It is not the disaster it could be, and the fix is ordinary: turn on lazy loading for the rest and stop sending a 2,791 pixel wide image into a 310 pixel wide box. Jewellery has to look sharp, which is the argument for serving the right size rather than the biggest one you have.

Nothing is cached

Every page tells browsers never to remember it

Your server sends no-store on every response, which means a returning customer downloads the whole site again from scratch, every visit. Your server takes about a second and a quarter to start answering, so that second visit feels exactly as slow as the first. One caching setting changes it.

05 What is already working

Your product pages tell people when the parcel arrives, and almost nobody does that

On the Ellie earrings page it says Delivery: Mon, Sep 14 to Tue, Sep 15. Actual dates, not "3 to 5 working days". That is the single most reassuring thing you can put on a product page and most shops your size never manage it. Do not let anyone talk you out of it.

Your checkout also lets people buy without making an account, which is rarer than it should be, and your phone, WhatsApp and email are all one tap away on every page. I am telling you this because a list of problems with no ending is useless. The shop works. What is wrong is a promise in a search result you are not keeping, and a payment option you are hiding.
