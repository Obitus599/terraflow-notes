01 For Sundar Meenakshi, via Jas Randhawa

Two photographs of salad weigh more than your entire ordering site

Jas passed me your way, so here it is. I went through saladstop.com.sg on a phone, the way somebody deciding on lunch would. It pulls 9.5 MB and two salad photos are 2.9 of that. Keeping the ordering system on its own site was a good decision, and I say why below.

02 Fix today

Costs you hungry people

Two photographs of salad are 2.9 megabytes of your homepage

Tuna San1.5 MB
Hail Caesar1.4 MB

Both are PNGs. PNG is a format for logos and screenshots, where every pixel has to be exact. For a photograph it stores far more than the eye can use. The same two images as WebP land at roughly a tenth of the size and nobody can tell the difference on a phone.

The whole homepage pulls 9.5 MB on an iPhone with an empty cache. Your customer is deciding what to eat, usually standing up, often on office wifi at noon. That is the worst possible moment to make somebody wait, and food photography is the one thing on the page you cannot afford to have arrive late.

03 Costing you orders

Invisible to search

None of your stores are described in a way Google can read

Your locations page lists where to find you. In the code behind it there is no restaurant data and no business location data of any kind, on that page or anywhere else on the site. Google is told you are an organisation with a website, and nothing about a single shop.

That is the data behind "salad near me" and the map pin with your hours on it. Somebody two streets from one of your stores searching at lunchtime is the easiest order you will ever take, and you are competing for it with one hand behind your back.

No main heading

Your homepage has fifteen subheadings and no heading

There is no H1 on the page. Fifteen H2s underneath it, but nothing at the top saying what this page is. That single tag is the strongest on-page signal there is, and on the homepage of a national salad brand it is empty.

It is also the thing a screen reader announces first. One line of code fixes both.

No way to call

Nobody can phone you

  • Homepageno phone
  • Locationsno phone
  • Contact usemail only
  • FAQemail only

There is not a single tappable phone number on the site, including the page that exists to tell people where your shops are. Somebody with a catering order for forty people, or a question about an allergy twenty minutes before lunch, has one option: send an email and wait.

If that is deliberate, and for a lot of chains it is, then the locations page should say so and point them somewhere. Right now it just looks like an oversight.

04 Worth tidying

Heavy for what it does

Five megabytes of code to show pictures and an Order Now button

  • JavaScript2.6 MB
  • Stylesheets2.4 MB
  • Largest single stylesheet529 KB
  • Images3.8 MB

Your marketing site is a set of pages that describe the food and then hand people over to the ordering system. It is carrying more code than most banking apps. Almost all of that is a page builder shipping every feature it has whether the page uses it or not, and most of it can be stripped without touching how anything looks.

05 What is already working

Keeping ordering on its own site was the right call

order.saladstop.com.sg answers in under half a second, against about one and a half seconds for the marketing site. Splitting them means the pages people browse can be as heavy as your marketing team likes without ever slowing down the screen where money changes hands. Plenty of chains put everything in one WordPress and regret it.

Your menu structure is also good. Breakfast, seasonals, protein bowls, create your own, all properly separated rather than dumped in one long list. I am telling you this because a list of problems with no ending is useless. The shape of the thing is right. What is wrong is weight, and a few pieces of information Google never receives.

06 The order I would do them in

  1. Convert the food photography to WebPStarting with the two that are 2.9 MB between them. An afternoon, and it is most of the 9.5.
  2. Add business data for every storeName, address, hours, phone, per location. This is the one that shows up as money in the map results.
  3. Put a real heading on the homepageOne line. It is the largest signal you are currently not sending.
  4. Decide about the phoneEither publish numbers on the locations page or say on it that everything goes through email.
  5. Strip the unused page builder codeHalf of the 5 MB is features these pages never use.
