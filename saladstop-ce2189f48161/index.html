1 Fix first

Delivery asks for a login before showing anything

↓
What it meansBefore anyone sees whether you deliver to them, what it costs or what's on the menu, they have to make an account. Someone choosing lunch has three other apps open. They pick the one that answers first.
The fixPickup already lets guests in, so the system can do it. Ask your ordering platform to let delivery guests enter an address and browse, and move the login to checkout.
A change on the ordering platform

2 Fix first

No order button on the first screen

What it meansPeople come to a salad chain's website to order. Make them scroll for the button and some of them won't.
The fixPin an "Order now" button to the bottom of the screen on phones, on every page.
About an hour

3 Worth doing

The order page opens on a store that's shut at weekends

What it meansOn a Saturday, the first thing your order page offers is a store that's closed. People have to know to change it.
The fixOpen on the nearest store that's open now, or on no store at all.
One setting, most likely

I converted both photos myself to check. Same size on screen, no difference you can see, a tenth of the weight. The whole home page pulls over 10 MB on a phone.

What it meansYour customer is deciding on lunch, often on office wifi at noon. Food photos that arrive late are the worst thing to make them wait for.
The fixSave the photos as WebP instead of PNG.
Thirty minutes

5 Worth doing

Your site and your app disagree on calories

What it meansPlenty of your customers count calories. Two numbers for one salad makes them wonder which other numbers are wrong.
The fixPick one source for calories and feed both the site and the app from it.
One source for both
