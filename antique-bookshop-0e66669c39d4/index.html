Five findings. The best line about the shop is the one nobody gets to read.

01 For Peter

Fifty years of bookselling, and the site tells phone visitors it's been five

I went through antiquebookshop.com.au the way a collector would: on a phone, looking for a book and then for the shop. The worst of it is that the page doesn't fit a phone, so everything on it shows up tiny. The basics a customer needs are all there and all correct, which is more than most shops manage.

02 Fix today

Costs visits now

The home page is nearly three phone screens wide

Phone screen390 px
Your home page1047 px

I opened the home page on a phone-sized screen. The row of four book photos is laid out as one fixed block 1039 pixels wide, so the phone shrinks the whole page to fit it. The result is text you have to pinch and zoom to read, on every line, including your hours and phone number.

Most people who look up a bookshop do it on a phone, often standing on the street deciding whether to come up. The fix is small: tell the page how wide a phone is and let that row of photos wrap onto two lines. Nothing else on the page has to change.

03 Losing you sales

Fifty becomes five

Your story is in a box that shows a fifth of it, and the part people see says "half a decade"

Your home page, in the scrolling box Peter has been selling antique and collectible books to libraries, dealers and interested individuals for nearly half a decade.

Half a decade is five years. The heading above it says over 50.

The introduction sits in a box 150 pixels tall with its own little scrollbar. About 80% of the text is hidden inside it, and on a phone the paragraph about your ANZAAB and ILAB membership is cut off mid-sentence. That paragraph is the reason a stranger trusts a dealer with a four-figure book, and almost nobody will scroll a box inside a page to find it.

The description Google shows under your name in search results says "established by Peter Tinslay in Sydney for 35 years". So a searcher sees 35, the heading says 50, and the story says five.

Sent elsewhere

"Search our inventory" sends buyers to AbeBooks, and the site never says how to buy from you

  • Search our inventoryopens AbeBooks in a new tab
  • Buy or order button on the sitenone
  • Catalogue 417a PDF, 2.7 MB
  • How to orderone line, by email or phone

Listing on AbeBooks is sensible, and it may be exactly how you want to sell. But anyone who clicks through lands on a marketplace full of other dealers. AbeBooks takes its commission on anything they buy, and if they don't buy there, they're one click from another shop's copy. The only route to buying straight from you is a single line saying orders can be left by email or phone.

If direct orders matter to you, a short "to buy a book, email or ring Peter" next to the search button, and on each catalogue, keeps those buyers with you. That's a sentence, not a shop.

Finding the door

The address is at the very bottom and doesn't open a map

Tyrrell's Building, Level 1, 328 Pacific Highway is in the footer only. It's plain text, so tapping it does nothing, and there's no map or directions link anywhere on the site. On a phone it's below the fold.

A first-floor shop is easy to walk past. One line near the top with the address and "Level 1", linked to Google Maps, gets people from the pavement to your stairs.

04 Worth tidying

Dead weight

A Google tracking script is the heaviest thing on the page

The home page loads 1.7 MB. The biggest single item is Google Tag Manager at 539 KB, bigger than any of your book photos. If nobody looks at the reports it feeds, removing it makes the page noticeably quicker on a phone. If someone does, keep it.

05 What is already working

Everything a customer needs to reach you is right

Sun Mon Tue Wed Thu Fri Sat

I checked on 21 October. Your hours (Tuesday to Saturday, 10am to 5pm) are clear and say when you're closed. The phone number dials when tapped. Your email address is there twice. The ANZAAB membership sits right at the top. Catalogue 417 is current, and the footer shows the site was updated on 2 October. I tested every link on the home page and none are broken. It loads in about two and a half seconds and is secure.

I am telling you this because a list of problems with no ending is useless. A site that's kept this current is the hard part, and everything above is about the page fitting on a phone and the story being readable.
