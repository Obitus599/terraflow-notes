02 Fix today

Costs you walk-ins

Your homepage is 18 megabytes of photographs

Your code, all of it0.3 MB
Your photos17.9 MB

I loaded the homepage on an iPhone with an empty cache. It pulled 18.2 MB across 77 requests, and 17.9 MB of that is photos. Ten of them are over half a megabyte on their own. The biggest is your signature dishes shot at 824 KB.

Read those two numbers again, because they are the good news. Your stylesheets and scripts together come to 0.2 MB. The site is not badly built. It is a well built site carrying eighteen megabytes of pictures, and the fix is the photos, not the website.

The person this hurts is the one standing on Cleveland Street deciding between you and the next place along. Resized and compressed properly, those same photos land under 2 MB and look identical on a phone.

03 Costing you tables

No way to book

Nobody can book a table on your website

Your homepage has a section headed RESERVE A TABLE. It says "Book your table in advance to avoid last minute rush", then "Alternatively, you can call 02 8399 3785". There is no alternative. The phone is the only option in that section, and the button underneath it says Know More rather than Book a table.

Reservations in your menu goes to a page titled Reserve your Table for Group Bookings, which opens with "Parties of up to 80 guests are welcome". Two people wanting a table on Friday read that heading and decide it is not for them. The form underneath also says you confirm by email, so even the people who do fill it in are not booked when they close the tab.

Delivery is the opposite. Menulog, Uber Eats and DoorDash sit in your top menu on every page and all three work. Somebody put real effort into the delivery side and nothing into the dining room.

Wasted first screen

The first thing on a phone is an award from 2016

Here is the whole first screen on an iPhone: your address bar, your logo, a photo of palak paneer, the words WELCOME TO MAYA DA DHABA, and underneath that, 2016 DIMMI People's Choice Award Winner.

That is the most valuable space you own and it is carrying a ten year old award from a booking site that no longer exists under that name. Nothing on that screen can be tapped to do anything. No menu, no book, no order. The award repeats across seven slides, so scrolling the carousel shows it again.

You have better things to put there. A dish and a price, or the one line that says what you are, would both work harder than a prize from 2016.

Cannot be tapped

The phone number at the top of every page is not a link

  • Yellow bar, top of every page, first thing anyone seesplain text
  • Call us today, further down the homepagetappable
  • Call us today, further down againtappable
  • 02 8399 3785 in the reserve sectiontappable

The number you put in front of everybody is the one that does nothing when you touch it. The three that work are 1,384, 2,413 and 2,946 pixels down the page. Since the phone is currently the only way to book a table, this is a bigger deal for you than it would be for most restaurants.

04 Worth tidying

Out of date

A heading from the pandemic is still on the homepage

Scroll down about three and a half thousand pixels and you reach "We're Fully Committed to your safety!". It is still marked up as the main heading of the page, which is the one signal search engines weigh most heavily on any page. Nobody has looked at the bottom half of this homepage in a long time and it shows.

Wrong signal

Your own code tells Google you are the cheapest kind of restaurant

The page title says Authentic Indian Fine Dining. The data underneath it, the part Google reads, sets your price range to a single dollar sign. That is the bottom of a four step scale, and it is what can appear next to you in search. Your entrees are 9.90 and up, so the number is wrong as well as unhelpful.

Same block of data still points at the http version of your address rather than https.

Invisible to Google

"What our guests say" has no reviews behind it

The heading is there. In the code there is no review data and no rating, so Google has nothing to show stars from. Restaurants that mark this up properly get a row of gold stars under their search result and you do not, which costs you clicks before anyone reaches the site at all.

05 What is already working

Your menu is done properly, and most restaurants get this wrong

Your menu is real text on a real page, with every dish and every price readable and selectable. Vegetable Samosa 9.90, right there in the code. Most places your size put the menu up as a PDF or a photograph, which means a phone has to pinch and zoom and Google cannot read a word of it. Yours can be read by both. Leave it exactly as it is.

Your address, your hours and your phone number are also marked up correctly, your images all carry alt text, and the site runs on very little code. I am telling you this because a list of problems with no ending is useless. The bones here are sound. What is wrong is the weight of the photos and the fact that nobody can book a table.

  4. Rename the reservations pageDrop "for Group Bookings" from the title so a table for two knows it is allowed.
